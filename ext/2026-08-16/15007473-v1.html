Calcite crystal during dissolution in aqueous solution
Fluorescence imaging, HPTS 510 nm emission under 405 and 488 nm excitation; higher pH produced weaker emission with 405 nm excitation and stronger emission with 488 nm excitation, while lower pH produced the opposite trend
Fluorescence imaging, Rhod-5N 580 nm emission under 550 nm excitation increased with Ca2+ concentration at constant pH and with pH at constant Ca2+ concentration; in calcium-free solution, fluorescence intensity did not vary with pH
Fluorescence imaging, pH calibration the 510 nm fluorescence intensity ratio under 488 and 405 nm excitation exhibited a sigmoidal dependence on pH; applicable pH range approximately 6.0–9.5
Fluorescence imaging, Ca2+ calibration normalized values followed the same curve regardless of pH; fitted constant Z = 437.23055 µM
Time-lapse fluorescence imaging immediately above the crystal surface pH increased from approximately 6 and stabilized at approximately 9; Ca2+ concentration rapidly increased to approximately 200 µM
Time-lapse fluorescence imaging around the crystal pH and Ca2+ concentrations increased concentrically; Ca2+ concentration increased at crystal protrusions, whereas the pH distribution became more concentric
