Zero-charge pinch-off point For the pristine DIB, lipid-coated droplets containing 100 mM KCl, 10 mM MOPS, and 2.6 mg/mL DPhPC were formed on two Ag/AgCl electrodes in 3:1 decane/hexadecane oil, contacted after monolayer stabilization, and equilibrated for approximately 5 min; a 100 mVpp, 0.0017 Hz sinusoidal voltage superimposed with a 500 mVpp, 10 Hz triangular voltage was applied across the membrane, capacitive membrane current was recorded continuously, and transferred charge obtained by numerical integration was plotted against slowly varying gate bias to obtain quasi-static Q–V(G) hysteresis; the zero-charge crossover is the applied gate bias at which net transferred charge is zero and provides a measure of the intrinsic potential offset across the membrane-device system; the approximately 0 V zero-charge crossover confirmed the absence of intrinsic membrane offset under symmetric electrolyte conditions; applying the same protocol with one lipid-coated droplet formed directly on the PEDOT:PSS channel shifted the pristine TCDB crossover to approximately −50 mV, indicating an intrinsic transmembrane potential of +50 mV generated by coupling the bilayer to the electrochemically active transistor and providing evidence of an effective internal gate bias beyond the externally applied voltage; for the 35 µM valinomycin-doped TCDB, the triangular-wave amplitude was reduced from 500 to 100 mVpp to prevent patch-clamp-amplifier saturation caused by the larger membrane currents resulting from ionophore-mediated K⁺ transport, while the low-frequency sinusoidal excitation was unchanged; its substantially larger hysteresis than the lipid-only TCDB and its asymmetry were observed; crossover points of approximately −50 to −70 mV indicated an intrinsic transmembrane potential of approximately +25 to +35 mV relative to the lipid-only TCDB
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy acquisition Biologic SP200 potentiostat; standalone droplet interface bilayers and transistor-coupled bilayers were measured in a two-terminal configuration between the Ag/AgCl gate electrode and grounded source contact over 0.1 Hz to 100 kHz with a 10 mV root-mean-square sinusoidal perturbation at open-circuit potential; spectra were fitted to the equivalent-circuit model using ZFit/OriginLab to extract solution resistance, membrane resistance, membrane capacitance, and PEDOT:PSS gate capacitance for each device configuration and valinomycin concentration; all electrical measurements were performed at 22 ± 1 °C, with instrument control and data acquisition using custom MATLAB and LabVIEW scripts unless otherwise stated
Bilayer formation confirmation Undoped transistor-coupled droplet bilayer without valinomycin; bilayer formation and confirmation used an Axopatch 200B patch-clamp amplifier in voltage-clamp mode operated with custom LabVIEW software; a 500 mVpp triangular function-generator waveform was attenuated by the patch-clamp amplifier to a 20 mVpp membrane voltage, with a 100 mV sinusoidal excitation superimposed; the periodic square-wave capacitive-current transient was characteristic of charging and discharging of an intact droplet interface bilayer and confirmed successful bilayer formation
