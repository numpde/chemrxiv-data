Free-standing bilayer electrochemical impedance spectroscopy For free-standing droplet interface bilayers containing varying concentrations of valinomycin, extracted membrane resistance decreased markedly with increasing valinomycin loading

Zero-charge crossover and intrinsic transmembrane potential For pristine free-standing DIBs under symmetric electrolyte conditions, the zero-charge crossover was approximately 0 V. Coupling one lipid-coated droplet directly to the PEDOT:PSS channel shifted the pristine TCDB crossover to approximately −50 mV, indicating an intrinsic transmembrane potential of +50 mV. For the 35 µM valinomycin-doped TCDB, crossover points of approximately −50 to −70 mV indicated an intrinsic transmembrane potential of approximately +25 to +35 mV relative to the lipid-only TCDB. Lipid-coated droplets contained 100 mM KCl, 10 mM MOPS, and 2.6 mg/mL DPhPC in 3:1 decane/hexadecane oil; quasi-static charge–voltage hysteresis used a 100 mVpp, 0.0017 Hz sinusoidal voltage with a 500 mVpp, 10 Hz triangular voltage, reduced to 100 mVpp for the valinomycin-doped TCDB
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy acquisition Biologic SP200 potentiostat; standalone droplet interface bilayers and transistor-coupled bilayers were measured in a two-terminal configuration between the Ag/AgCl gate electrode and grounded source contact over 0.1 Hz to 100 kHz with a 10 mV root-mean-square sinusoidal perturbation at open-circuit potential; spectra were fitted to the equivalent-circuit model using ZFit/OriginLab to extract solution resistance, membrane resistance, membrane capacitance, and PEDOT:PSS gate capacitance for each device configuration and valinomycin concentration; all electrical measurements were performed at 22 ± 1 °C, with instrument control and data acquisition using custom MATLAB and LabVIEW scripts unless otherwise stated
