PEDOT:PSS organic electrochemical transistor
Device geometry and channel thickness Channel thickness 140–270 nm owing to fabrication variations and greater than the 100 nm gold-electrode thickness in every device
Bare-transistor impedance parameters Leakage resistance = 0.86–1 GΩ; channel capacitance = 0.153 µF; solution resistance = 1275 Ω; a PDMS well over the channel was filled with 100 mM KCl containing 10 mM MOPS and gated with an Ag/AgCl electrode; impedance measurements used a potentiostat in a two-terminal configuration between the gate electrode and grounded source contact over 0.1 Hz–100 kHz with a 10 mV root-mean-square sinusoidal perturbation at open-circuit potential; the Main prints “Biologic SP200 potentiostat,” whereas the SI prints “BioLogic potentiostat”; impedance magnitude and phase were fitted simultaneously to the equivalent-circuit model using ZFit/OriginLab; all electrical measurements were performed at 22 ± 1 °C, with instrument control and data acquisition using custom MATLAB and LabVIEW scripts unless otherwise stated
Direct-current leakage validation Gate-voltage steps of −100, −50, 0, +50, and +100 mV were each held for 300 s, with return to 0 V for 60 s between steps to minimize residual polarization effects; gate current was recorded continuously; after the initial transient associated with capacitive charging of the PEDOT:PSS channel decayed, the final 60 s of each current trace were averaged; leakage current depended approximately linearly on applied gate voltage and was fitted as I(leak) = G(leak)V(G) + I(offset), where G(leak) is the effective leakage conductance and I(offset) accounts for any residual baseline current, with R(leak) = 1/G(leak); the fit yielded an effective leakage resistance of approximately 0.86 GΩ with R² approximately 0.96, in good agreement with the approximately 1 GΩ EIS estimate; because the steady-state leakage currents were on the order of pA to low nA and susceptible to baseline drift, instrument offset, and low-frequency noise, the resistance was interpreted as an effective estimate rather than an exact measurement

Valinomycin-functionalized droplet interface bilayer
Transistor-coupled bilayer electrochemical impedance spectroscopy Without valinomycin, nominal membrane resistance was greater than 10 GΩ; increasing the valinomycin concentration to 65 µM progressively decreased membrane resistance by as much as 215-fold; area-normalized specific membrane resistance also decreased, confirming that enhanced ionic conductivity originates from increased membrane permeability rather than differences in membrane area; specific membrane capacitance remained nearly unchanged, indicating that enhanced ionic permeability occurred without substantial alteration of the bilayer’s dielectric structure or effective thickness
Free-standing bilayer electrochemical impedance spectroscopy For free-standing droplet interface bilayers containing varying concentrations of valinomycin, extracted membrane resistance decreased markedly with increasing valinomycin loading, consistent with enhanced K⁺ transport across the bilayer
Transistor-coupled bilayer electrical properties Valinomycin concentration, area, specific resistance, specific capacitance: 0 µM, (3.94 ± 0.26) × 10⁻⁴ cm², 5.63 × 10³ kΩ·cm², 0.5 µF/cm²; 20 µM, (2.47 ± 0.44) × 10⁻⁴ cm², 54.1 kΩ·cm², 0.511 µF/cm²; 35 µM, (2.3866 ± 0.47) × 10⁻⁴ cm², 23.866 kΩ·cm², 0.540 µF/cm²; 65 µM, (2.66 ± 0.5) × 10⁻⁴ cm², 7.714 kΩ·cm², 0.511 µF/cm²; the source prints the specific-resistance unit with a hyphen

Zero-charge pinch-off point For the pristine DIB, lipid-coated droplets containing 100 mM KCl, 10 mM MOPS, and 2.6 mg/mL DPhPC were formed on two Ag/AgCl electrodes in 3:1 decane/hexadecane oil, contacted after monolayer stabilization, and equilibrated for approximately 5 min; a 100 mVpp, 0.0017 Hz sinusoidal voltage superimposed with a 500 mVpp, 10 Hz triangular voltage was applied across the membrane, capacitive membrane current was recorded continuously, and transferred charge obtained by numerical integration was plotted against slowly varying gate bias to obtain quasi-static Q–V(G) hysteresis; the zero-charge crossover is the applied gate bias at which net transferred charge is zero and provides a measure of the intrinsic potential offset across the membrane-device system; the approximately 0 V zero-charge crossover confirmed the absence of intrinsic membrane offset under symmetric electrolyte conditions; applying the same protocol with one lipid-coated droplet formed directly on the PEDOT:PSS channel shifted the pristine TCDB crossover to approximately −50 mV, indicating an intrinsic transmembrane potential of +50 mV generated by coupling the bilayer to the electrochemically active transistor and providing evidence of an effective internal gate bias beyond the externally applied voltage; for the valinomycin-doped TCDB, the triangular-wave amplitude was reduced from 500 to 100 mVpp to prevent patch-clamp-amplifier saturation caused by the larger membrane currents resulting from ionophore-mediated K⁺ transport, while the low-frequency sinusoidal excitation was unchanged; its substantially larger Q–V(G) hysteresis demonstrated enhanced charge accumulation arising from selective K⁺ transport across the membrane, and its asymmetry reflected the voltage-dependent nature of ion transport through the valinomycin-containing membrane; crossover points of approximately −50 to −70 mV indicated an intrinsic transmembrane potential of approximately +25 to +35 mV relative to the lipid-only TCDB
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy acquisition Biologic SP200 potentiostat; standalone droplet interface bilayers and transistor-coupled bilayers were measured in a two-terminal configuration between the Ag/AgCl gate electrode and grounded source contact over 0.1 Hz–100 kHz with a 10 mV root-mean-square sinusoidal perturbation at open-circuit potential; spectra were fitted to the equivalent-circuit model using ZFit/OriginLab to extract solution resistance, membrane resistance, membrane capacitance, and PEDOT:PSS gate capacitance for each device configuration and valinomycin concentration; all electrical measurements were performed at 22 ± 1 °C, with instrument control and data acquisition using custom MATLAB and LabVIEW scripts unless otherwise stated
Bilayer formation confirmation Undoped transistor-coupled droplet bilayer without valinomycin; bilayer formation and confirmation used an Axopatch 200B patch-clamp amplifier in voltage-clamp mode operated with custom LabVIEW software; a 500 mVpp triangular function-generator waveform was attenuated by the patch-clamp amplifier to a 20 mVpp membrane voltage, with a 100 mV sinusoidal excitation superimposed; the periodic square-wave capacitive-current transient was characteristic of charging and discharging of an intact droplet interface bilayer and confirmed successful bilayer formation
