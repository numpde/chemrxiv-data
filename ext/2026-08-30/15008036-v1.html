Asymmetric droplet interface bilayers
Experimental configuration for each experiment; two lipid-monolayer-coated aqueous droplets suspended in hexadecane from Ag/AgCl-tipped microtip electrodes; current-amplifier-headstage droplet defined as cis and grounded droplet as trans; positive current defined as cation flow from cis to trans; unless otherwise noted, each droplet contained DPhPC- or DoPhPC-based fusogenic liposomes with DOPE, POPS, and cholesterol at 50:10:20:20 mol % in 100 mM KCl, 10 mM MOPS, and 6 mM CaCl₂, pH 7.4 titrated with NaOH Asymmetric droplet interface bilayers formed by contacting the two lipid-monolayer-coated droplets; the trans leaflet contained DPhPC-based fusogenic lipids and the cis leaflet contained DoPhPC-based fusogenic lipids; after bilayer formation, DPhPC-based fusogenic liposomes were microinjected into the trans droplet
Lipid formulations; DPhPC- or DoPhPC-based fusogenic mixture with DOPE, POPS, and cholesterol at 50:10:20:20 molar ratio; baseline DPhPC-only or DoPhPC-only films Final fusogenic lipid concentration 3 mg/mL (4.18 mM); baseline films 2 mg/mL, corresponding to 2.34 mM DPhPC or 2.45 mM DoPhPC
Electrical monitoring of bilayer formation and stability; 10 mV, 10 Hz triangular voltage waveform applied across the membrane; bilayer modeled as a parallel resistor and capacitor Resistance and capacitance were extracted by fitting the square-wave current response to the parallel-RC circuit model; the capacitance–voltage curve across applied DC voltages was fit to a quadratic function to determine Vₘᵢₙ, the voltage at minimum capacitance, and Cₘᵢₙ, the corresponding minimum capacitance
Empirical relationship between nominal bilayer capacitance C and applied voltage V C = C₀(1 + α(V + Vᵣ)²), where C₀ is capacitance at V = 0, α is the voltage-dependent fractional capacitance increase from electrowetting and electrocompression, and Vᵣ is the intrinsic membrane potential defined as the total difference between the dipole and surface potentials of the opposing lipid leaflets; capacitance is minimized at V = −Vᵣ
Bilayer integrity during and immediately after microinjection, 75 droplet interface bilayers Membrane resistance remained above 1 GΩ, indicating that microinjection does not substantially disrupt the pre-formed bilayer
Capacitance–voltage acquisition and analysis; 10 mV, 10 Hz triangular AC waveform superimposed on DC steps in 25 mV increments, each held for 20 s to allow membrane capacitance to equilibrate; steady-state current waveform from each DC step fit to a parallel-RC equivalent circuit to extract the corresponding capacitance value; before data collection, a priming sweep from 0 to +250 mV pre-conditioned the membrane, followed by a continuous descending sweep from +250 to −100 mV during which all C–V data were collected; capacitance at each step normalized to zero-volt membrane capacitance (C/C₀) to account for differences in nominal bilayer area among separate DIBs; resulting C–V curves fit with a quadratic polynomial to extract Vₘᵢₙ Vₘᵢₙ = V(min(C)), the applied voltage at minimum capacitance, provided a direct label-free readout of intrinsic membrane potential and lipid-leaflet asymmetry
Reference-bilayer Vₘᵢₙ: symmetric DPhPC|DPhPC; symmetric DoPhPC|DoPhPC; fully asymmetric DPhPC|DoPhPC (trans|cis), with Vₘᵢₙ = |Vᵣ|; fully asymmetric DoPhPC|DPhPC (trans|cis), with Vₘᵢₙ = |Vᵣ| −1.5 ± 2.3 mV; −2.9 ± 3.1 mV; +135.3 ± 5.4 mV; −133.4 ± 3.5 mV, respectively; the two symmetric bilayers confirmed near-zero net membrane potentials (Vᵣ ∼ 0); the sign depended on which lipid leaflet was connected to the headstage electrode versus the ground electrode
