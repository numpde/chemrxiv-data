The feature below 200 nm arises from high-energy π → π* transitions of isolated C=C bonds alongside σ → σ* or σ → π* transitions of non-conjugated groups; the 250 nm feature is attributed to π → π* transitions within the delocalized sp2 core framework; the 300–350 nm feature arises from n → π* transitions associated with surface functional groups and edge states
Fluorescence Broad fluorescence across the 300–500 nm emission window. 3D excitation–emission spectra revealed three distinct UV fluorophore features at λ(ex)/λ(em) approximately 230/360 nm, 275/400 nm, and 325/400 nm. These results indicate that the 250 nm UV–Vis shoulder is a convolution of absorption bands centered at 230 and 275 nm. The 230 and 275 nm centers had Stokes shifts of 1.95 and 1.41 eV, respectively, indicating core-related electronic transitions. The high-energy excitation band at 230 nm was attributed to direct π → π* transitions within the conjugated aromatic core; the 275 nm band was assigned to π → π* transitions involving surface edge states conjugated with oxygen-containing functional groups, specifically delocalized π-domains directly bound to surface oxygen heteroatoms. The final feature is subsequently described at 320 nm excitation, rather than 325 nm, with a Stokes shift of approximately 0.71 eV and a similar emission profile; it can likewise be attributed to edge-state transitions involving the same boundary-delocalized bonds within the core’s π-domains and their conjugated surface oxygen-containing functional groups

Electrophoretic mobility and zeta-potential trends Electrophoretic mobility measurements revealed distinct differences in surface charge between the unmodified and functionalized samples. Surface modification decreased the overall magnitude of zeta potential in both series, shifting values toward more positive potential. ACD1 modifications gave modest positive shifts of 5 to 10 mV, except ACD1-C8, which the source says “exhibited virtually same mobilities” as unmodified particles; ACD2 showed more pronounced shifts of approximately 10 to 15 mV
Surface-tension trends Modified ACD1 samples showed higher overall surface activity than the ACD2 series. Samples treated with 1-butanol showed “negligible deviations from the unmodified control (ACD2_C4)” or a slight surface-tension increase for ACD1_C4; the source’s parenthetical is retained without resolving its mapping. Surface tension decreased systematically with increasing alkyl chain length, reaching 34–36 mN·m⁻¹ for C5 modification, with only slight further reductions for longer chains. Physical-mixture blank controls of carbon dots and alkanols showed no significant surface-tension change from unmodified particles. Covalent grafting cannot be confirmed with absolute certainty
XPS elemental composition XPS elemental analysis revealed pronounced structural differences between the ACD1 and ACD2 series. Except for the octanol-modified sample ACD1_C8, ACD1 had high carbon contents of 73.5–80.1 % with relatively low oxygen and sulphur heteroatom fractions; ACD2 contained 56.0–62.1 % carbon and was significantly more heteroatom-rich. Sulphur atomic percentages were comparable between series; ACD2’s elevated heteroatom fraction stemmed primarily from oxygen content 1.5 to 2.0 times higher than that of ACD1. ACD1 carbon content varied slightly and non-systematically without correlation to alkanol chain length; no significant unmodified-versus-modified differences were observed except for ACD1_C8, which had markedly lower carbon and higher heteroatom levels resembling ACD2. High-resolution XPS S 2p Deconvolution of the high-resolution S 2p spectra revealed two primary species: sulfite-like groups (–SO₂–O, including sulfonate esters) at approximately 168.3 eV and sulfate-like groups (–SO₃–O, including sulfate esters) at approximately 169.1 eV. Both the ACD1 and ACD2 series originally contained a higher proportion of sulfite-like groups; surface modification clearly increased sulfate-like functionalities in both series, indicating significant oxidation during treatment. This oxidation was particularly prominent in ACD1_C8, where sulfate-like species exceeded sulfite-like groups by more than an order of magnitude, confirming that its substantial oxygen increase reflects sulfur-bound oxygen within sulfate groups rather than deep carbon oxidation, consistent with minimal C=O
ATR-FTIR Spectra exhibit straightforward profiles confirming the presence of various functional groups. In the high-wavenumber region, a broad absorption band spans 3200 to 3500 cm⁻¹. Cross-referencing these findings with XPS analysis allows this broad feature to be assigned primarily to O–H stretching vibrations of surface hydroxyl groups and protonated sulfate and sulfonate functionalities. The regions between 2850 and 2960 cm⁻¹ and 1380 and 1500 cm⁻¹ correspond to characteristic C–H stretching and C–H bending vibrations of aliphatic methylene and methyl groups, respectively. Aromatic C–H stretching associated with the graphitic core, typically at 3000–3100 cm⁻¹, cannot be cleanly resolved here due to extensive overlapping with the intense O–H stretching envelope. In the lower-wavenumber region, a prominent 1790–1550 cm⁻¹ envelope centered near 1650 cm⁻¹ comprises C=C stretching from aromatic and conjugated domains at 1600–1650 cm⁻¹ and a higher-frequency shoulder at 1650–1790 cm⁻¹ that reflects C=O stretching vibrations of carbonyl functionalities, primarily ketones and minor carboxyl groups. XPS indicates relatively low carbonyl content in all samples, particularly ACD1_C6 and ACD1_C8. Because the composite peak’s overall profile and intensity remain largely unchanged across the series, C=C stretching vibrations clearly represent its dominant contribution. Intense overlapping bands at 1120–1300 and 1000–1090 cm⁻¹ arise from asymmetric and symmetric S=O stretching of sulfate and sulfonate groups and their ester derivatives, substantiated by characteristic S–O bending near 600 cm⁻¹. C–O stretching vibrations from alcohol and potential ether functionalities also fall within 1000–1300 cm⁻¹, but severe overlap with the stronger S=O modes obscures their individual resolution; the secondary-alcohol C–O region can be identified with reasonable certainty as a faint shoulder centered near 1100 cm⁻¹. Comparing the baseline spectra of the ACD1 and ACD2 series reveals several clear structural differences: in the ACD2 series, the aliphatic-C–H/broad-O–H intensity ratio is lower, the peak near 1650 cm⁻¹ is visibly diminished, and ACD2 exhibits a more pronounced secondary-alcohol C–O shoulder near 1100 cm⁻¹. For the ACD1 series, modification produces no systematic variations in the high-wavenumber region (4000 to 2500 cm⁻¹). The ACD2 series shows a clear, systematic increase in integrated aliphatic C–H stretching intensity that scales directly with alkanol alkyl chain length and peaks for ACD2_C8. Across both series, functionalized samples display an enhancement in the S=O stretching bands attributed to sulfate esters, appearing near 1240 and 1080 cm⁻¹. The most pronounced spectral shift occurs in the ACD1_C8 sample, where these sulfate ester bands increase dramatically
