Bovine carbonic anhydrase II with acetazolamide
BCAII and AZM sample conditions for apparent K(d) and in-droplet HDX-MS measurements BCAII was dissolved in 100 mM ammonium acetate to make a concentration of 10 µM; the stock was diluted using 100 mM AA as required (10 nM) for the nMS apparent equilibrium dissociation constant (K(d)) determinations, while the 10 µM stock was employed directly for in-droplet HDX-MS experiments; AZM stock was prepared in DMSO at 10 mM and diluted as required to comprise no more than 3 % DMSO of the total volume of the protein-ligand sample; For each sample, the ligand was incubated with the protein for approximately 2 minutes before being sprayed into the MS inlet
Multidevice capillary vibrating sharp-edge spray ionization conditions for in-droplet HDX A waveform generator (Tektronix AFG-1062, Beaverton, OR, USA) and an amplifier (Krohn-Hite 7500, Brockton, MA, USA) were used to actuate two cVSSI devices; analyte and reagent solutions were infused into the appropriate devices using the same syringe pump at 10 µL/min; emitter points were at a printed "90°" angle; the analyte emitter tip was positioned comparatively farther away (~4 mm) than the D₂O-reagent emitter tip (~1 mm), facing directly into the mass spectrometer inlet; +1300 V bias voltage was applied to the analyte solution; No voltage was applied to the reagent (D₂O) solution. First, the cVSSI device generating the D₂O reagent plume was turned off and the analyte device was turned on; mass spectra were recorded for analyte ions that were not deuterated. Then, both devices were turned on to initiate in-droplet HDX. Data recorded for the deuterated ions as well
Mass-spectrometry instrument, acquisition, and signal-averaging settings Q-Exactive hybrid quadrupole orbitrap mass spectrometer from Thermo Fisher Scientific (San Jose, CA, U.S.A.); to identify biomolecules in a low charge state, the scan range was increased to 5000 m/z; ion transfer tube temperature was set to 300 °C in the positive ion mode experiments; AGC target 1 × 10⁶; complete scan resolution 140000; for K(d) and deuterium uptake, signal-averaged mass spectra were generated using xCalibur Qual Browser
Unbound-protein contribution correction for the AZM-bound hydrogen-deuterium exchange spectrum The HDX spectrum of the untreated protein was scaled to match the intensity of the corresponding AZM-treated HDX spectrum, and the scaled unbound protein contribution was subtracted from the AZM-treated HDX spectrum to obtain a corrected spectrum for the AZM-bound protein. Scaling factor S = I(Unbound, AZM treated) / I(Unbound, AZM untreated). The scaling factor was then multiplied by every intensity value in the unbound-protein HDX spectrum. At each m/z data point, I(HDX, AZM bound corrected, i) = I(HDX, AZM-treated, i) − S × I(HDX, Unbound, i), where I(HDX, AZM-treated, i) is the measured intensity at the i’th m/z point in the AZM-treated HDX spectrum, containing contributions from both unbound and AZM-bound protein, I(HDX, Unbound, i) is the protein-without-AZM intensity at the same m/z point, S is the scaling factor used to match the unbound-protein contribution in the two spectra, and I(HDX, AZM bound corrected, i) is the corrected intensity assigned to the AZM-bound protein
Number of incorporated deuteriums from corrected spectral intensities Main: the number of deuterium exchanges was determined by measuring the shift in weighted isotope m/z value, m/z(w), upon HDX reaction using Equation (1). Binding-associated percent-reduction calculation Weighted m/z values are calculated for before and after HDX to determine the number of incorporated deuterium; percent reductions are reported from three independent measurements. N(D) = Number of incorporated deuterium. Percent reduction = [N(D,Unbound) − N(D,Bound)] / N(D,Bound) × 100. All numerical values are presented to two decimal places. Positive ΔD values indicate difference in deuterium incorporation in the AZM-bound protein than in the unbound protein
Decrease in deuterium uptake upon AZM binding AZM-binding to BCAII decreased the deuterium uptake by 18.71 ± 1.04, 11.99 ± 0.81, 16.19 ± 0.87, and 13.73 ± 0.66 deuterium’s for the 7⁺, 8⁺, 9⁺, and 10⁺ charge states, respectively. Overall, a reduction of 22.51 %, 14.95 %, 21.68 %, and 15.86 % was observed for the 7⁺, 8⁺, 9⁺, and 10⁺ charge states, respectively. Although the magnitude of the HDX reactivity response varied among different charged species, consistent reduction in reactivity was observed for all charge states. The relative reduction was highest for 7⁺ and 9⁺ ions and somewhat smaller for 8⁺ and 10⁺ ions. This consistent decrease in deuterium incorporation across the examined charge states indicates AZM-binding reduces the global HDX reactivity. The findings of the in-droplet HDX-MS are consistent with ligand induced conformational stabilization of the protein. The percentage reduction was calculated independently for each replicate as (U − B)/B × 100, where U and B are the mean numbers of incorporated deuterium atoms for unbound and AZM-bound BCAII, respectively; the mean percentage reduction from three independent measurements (n=3) is reported. In the model system of BCAII and its inhibitor AZM, a significant reduction in deuterium incorporation was observed, indicating conformational stabilization and reduced structural plasticity in the complex compared to apo protein

Fraction-bound calculation for apparent K(d) Fraction bound is determined from the relative abundance of free and ligand-bound charge states using Equation 2 and the features from the mass spectrum of the protein-ligand sample: fraction bound = Σₙ I(PL) / [Σₙ I(P) + Σₙ I(PL)], where Σₙ I(P) is the sum of the intensity of the unbound protein ions and Σₙ I(PL) is the sum of the intensity of the ligand-bound protein ions for the n⁺ charge state, with n = 8 and 9; only the 8⁺ and 9⁺ charge states were considered because they were clearly resolved and consistently detected across the whole ligand concentrations in the titration
Single-site Langmuir binding-isotherm fit Y = B(max)[L] / [K(d) + [L]], where Y = Predicted values, B(max) = Maximum fraction bound, [L] = Ligand concentration, and K(d) = Apparent binding affinity / Equilibrium dissociation constant; K(d) values are obtained by fitting the mass-spectrometry-derived fraction bound to the non-linear binding isotherm
