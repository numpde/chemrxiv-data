SnTe@S, SnTe@P, and SnTe@V
XRD All three samples showed sharp Bragg reflections indexed to cubic rock-salt Fm3̅m, JCPDS No. 08-0487, with no impurity peaks detected, confirming phase purity. For the (220) reflection: SnTe@S, broadest peak, FWHM = 1944 arcsec at 2θ = 28.22°, reflecting smaller crystallite size and higher disorder; SnTe@P, narrower peak, FWHM = 985.5 arcsec at 2θ = 28.26°, consistent with larger crystallite size; SnTe@V, sharpest reflection, FWHM = 695.1 arcsec at 2θ = 28.36°, evidencing enhanced crystallinity and lattice modification induced by V incorporation. The 0.10° (360 arcsec) shift toward a higher angle in SnTe@V indicated unit-cell contraction
XRD acquisition High-resolution X-ray diffraction with Cu Kα radiation, λ = 1.5406 Å
XPS Survey spectra confirmed Sn and Te as dominant constituents in all samples; the V 3p feature, expected at 32–46 eV, was weak in wide scans because of overlap and low dopant concentration, while high-resolution scans unambiguously confirmed V incorporation. Sn 3d: doublet at 485.6 eV (Sn²⁺ in SnTe) and higher-energy peak at 487.3 eV (Sn⁴⁺ from surface SnO₂). Te 3d: 571.5 eV (Te²⁻ in SnTe) and 575.6 eV (Te⁴⁺ from TeO₂), confirming partial surface oxidation. SnTe@S and SnTe@V both exhibited systematic positive core-level shifts relative to SnTe@P. The high-resolution V 3p spectrum was broad and asymmetric, with contributions from V²⁺, V³⁺, V⁴⁺, and V⁵⁺, confirming successful incorporation of V into the SnTe lattice; the orbital-component labels immediately before the four Sn/Te binding energies are not rendered in the PDF

TEM particle morphology and size All samples showed quasi-spherical nanoparticles; Gaussian-fitted mean particle sizes were 26.4 ± 18 nm for SnTe@S, 49.2 ± 13 nm for SnTe@P, and 52.2 ± 12 nm for SnTe@V. SnTe@S showed aggregated grains and numerous grain boundaries, indicating a polycrystalline disordered structure; SnTe@P showed smoother surfaces; SnTe@V displayed the sharpest lattice fringes and most ordered facets; high-resolution TEM showed well-defined lattice fringes consistent with SnTe planes, JCPDS 08-0487

Low-temperature ESR acquisition Main p. 5 describes low-temperature magnetoconductance measurements at 7 K using X-band (9.7 GHz) electron paramagnetic resonance spectroscopy; Main pp. 9–10 call the technique ESR and specify continuous-wave operation and zero-field conditions. The signal, proportional to the first derivative of the cavity quality factor Q with respect to magnetic field, dQ/dB, was obtained by modulating the magnetic field under lock-in detection conditions. The Q factor reflects sample conductivity and energy absorption, making signal intensity linearly dependent on conductivity variations. Magnetoconductivity was obtained by integrating differential magnetoconductivity data from the ESR spectra; integrated ESR absorption is proportional to the change in electrical conductivity, Δσ, and thus to conductance, ΔG; differential and integrated magnetoconductivity curves were fitted with the Hikami–Larkin–Nagaoka model
Hikami–Larkin–Nagaoka model Equation 4 is printed as Δσ(B) = −αe²/(πh)[ln(B(φ)/B) − ψ(1/2 + B(φ)/B)], where α is the WAL prefactor, Ψ is the digamma function, e is the electronic charge, and h is Planck’s constant; the equation uses lowercase ψ while the following definition uses uppercase Ψ. The source prints B(φ) = h/(8πeHl(φ)) as the characteristic magnetic field, where H is the applied magnetic field and l(φ) is the phase-coherence length; this printed expression is retained as given
ESR-derived magnetoconductance and weak antilocalization The WAL feature appeared as a cusp-like negative magnetoconductance signature originating from topological surface states. Hikami–Larkin–Nagaoka fits at 7 K gave (prefactor α, phase-coherence length l(φ)): SnTe@S, (0.55 ± 0.1, 5.5 ± 1.5 nm); SnTe@P, (0.73 ± 0.12, 7.2 ± 1.8 nm); SnTe@V, (1.8 ± 0.2, 12.5 ± 2.5 nm). SnTe@V showed the most pronounced cusp, largest α, and longest l(φ), indicating robust multi-channel WAL and stabilized, highly coherent topological surface states; the cover page does not render the coherence-length symbol, while the manuscript title page prints l(φ)
