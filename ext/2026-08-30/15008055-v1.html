Variable-temperature MAS NMR Linewidth and relaxation analyses yield consistent hopping barriers and an Arrhenius-extrapolated room-temperature bulk halide diffusivity of order 10⁻¹¹ cm² s⁻¹. Because the extrapolation spans structural phase transitions, the value is treated as an order-of-magnitude estimate rather than a direct room-temperature measurement
Variable-temperature MAS NMR composition series Variable-temperature MAS NMR was used for CsPbBr₃, CsPb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃, (Cs₀.₇₅Rb₀.₂₅)PbBr₃, and (Cs₀.₇₅Rb₀.₂₅)Pb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃
MAS NMR acquisition Variable-temperature ¹³³Cs and ²⁰⁷Pb MAS NMR spectra were acquired at 20.0 T and 4 kHz MAS
¹³³Cs MAS NMR phase-transition signatures For CsPbBr₃, the temperature dependence of the isotropic ¹³³Cs shift and linewidth reveals two known phase transitions and supports the existence of an additional low-temperature transition previously proposed from diffraction. With compositional mixing, static disorder substantially broadens the room-temperature ¹³³Cs resonances, masking some spectral discontinuities that would otherwise mark phase transitions directly; the broadening reflects a distribution of local environments created by A-site or X-site substitution
¹³³Cs spin-lattice relaxation In all four compositions, ¹³³Cs spin-lattice relaxation time T₁ reveals distinct minima associated with structural phase transitions, although these are not observed for every phase transition. For the three materials in which the high-temperature transition can already be assigned from one-dimensional spectra, the transition temperatures coincide with T₁ minima. For (Cs₀.₇₅Rb₀.₂₅)Pb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃, where static disorder makes straightforward spectral assignment difficult, T₁ clearly captures the high-temperature transition
²⁰⁷Pb MAS NMR of CsPbBr₃ Variable-temperature ²⁰⁷Pb MAS NMR provides an early indication of rapidly changing dynamics: scalar (J) coupling between lead and bromine disappears at elevated temperatures, indicating loss of a static Pb–Br bonding environment on the NMR timescale
¹³³Cs motional narrowing and hopping barriers For CsPb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃, the ¹³³Cs signal is broad at room temperature and narrows very substantially on heating, yielding an activation energy for halide hopping of about 0.34 eV by linewidth analysis. For (Cs₀.₇₅Rb₀.₂₅)PbBr₃, A-site disorder broadens the low-temperature signal, but narrowing remains incomplete even at the highest temperatures measured, indicating that A-site diffusion is still comparatively slow. For (Cs₀.₇₅Rb₀.₂₅)Pb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃, the much broader room-temperature ¹³³Cs line undergoes nearly complete narrowing at high temperature in two regimes corresponding to two independently resolved motional averaging processes. The lower-temperature regime yields the same halide activation energy found for CsPb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃, suggesting that halide diffusion is largely insensitive to A-site composition; the higher-temperature regime yields an A-site hopping barrier of around 0.8 eV, attributed to motion in the A-site sublattice. These observations collectively show that in this system halide mixing accelerates both halide and A-site diffusion
High-temperature ¹³³Cs T₁ minimum In the two mixed-halide compositions, but not in the bromide-only materials, ¹³³Cs T₁ exhibits a second high-temperature minimum that does not correspond to a phase transition. For ¹³³Cs at 20 T, this minimum corresponds to motion on the nanosecond timescale and is most consistently attributed to halide diffusion. Its appearance at nearly the same temperature in CsPb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃ and (Cs₀.₇₅Rb₀.₂₅)Pb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃ reinforces the linewidth-analysis conclusion that halide diffusion depends primarily on X-site rather than A-site composition
¹³³Cs spin-spin relaxation In (Cs₀.₇₅Rb₀.₂₅)Pb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃, T₂′ exhibits a lower- and a higher-temperature minimum, attributed respectively to halide hopping and A-site cation hopping. The higher-temperature minimum yields an activation energy of 0.77 eV, close to the 0.84 eV obtained from linewidth analysis. In CsPb(Br₀.₅I₀.₅)₃, only the lower-temperature minimum is observed within the accessible temperature window
