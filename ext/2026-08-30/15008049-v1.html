PVA/TiO₂/microcrystalline-cellulose biofilm
FTIR Before dye adsorption: broad peak within 3400–3200 cm⁻¹, specifically 3291 cm⁻¹ (abundant –OH groups from PVA); small peak at 2925 cm⁻¹ (asymmetric stretching of aliphatic C–H in the sp³-hybridized methylene groups of the PVA backbone); sharpest peak at 1710 cm⁻¹ (C=O ester bonds and carboxyl C=O groups, indicating successful crosslinking between PVA and citric acid); broad envelope between 1150 and 1300 cm⁻¹ with maximum at 1208 cm⁻¹ (C–O stretching of the ester linkage formed between the citric acid carboxyl groups and the PVA hydroxyls), consistent with the strong carbonyl band at 1710 cm⁻¹; no distinct Ti–O peak was observed, and the FTIR could not clearly distinguish between cellulose and PVA due to overlapping functional groups. After dye adsorption: new absorption at 1642 cm⁻¹ appears only after dye treatment and is assigned to the azo (–N=N–) chromophore; change at 1380 cm⁻¹ assigned to S=O stretching of the Avitera Light Red SE sulfonate groups (–SO₃⁻), whose appearance after treatment indicates that the sulfonate groups are present on the film; persistent C=O ester peak at 1710 cm⁻¹ confirms permanent esterification crosslinking between citric acid and PVA
FTIR acquisition PerkinElmer Spectrum-4600 (Model: Spectrum 2) at the Dyes and Chemical Engineering (DCE) Lab, BUTEX; 4000–400 cm⁻¹, covering the mid-infrared range
XRD 22–23° suggested the presence of the highly crystalline (200) lattice plane of cellulose within the biofilm matrix; 19–20° (PVA lattice parameter, partially overlapping cellulose peaks and demonstrating successful composite development); the relatively reduced biofilm intensity might be associated with incorporation of microcrystalline cellulose; 27.4° and 54.3° ((110) and (211) planes of rutile TiO₂, confirming incorporation of the oxide and its polymorph); no reflection at 25.3°, the principal (101) anatase peak, indicating that the nanoparticles used here are predominantly rutile
XRD acquisition Malvern Panalytical Empyrean X-ray diffractometer; Cu Kα radiation, λ = 1.5406 Å; accelerating voltage 45 kV; tube current 40 mA; 2θ = 10–80°; step size 0.01°; continuous scanning mode
FE-SEM morphology Before dye adsorption, the biofilm had smooth and uniform surfaces with minimal irregularities, suggesting a low degree of roughness; minor irregularities from uneven MCC distribution were detected; MCC structures were clearly observed on the biofilm surface, which was nonporous and dense. After dye adsorption, the surface became non-uniform, with irregular adhesion of dye particles clearly visible; TiO₂ was largely obscured in FESEM analysis due to the low amount of TiO₂ nanoparticles used
FE-SEM acquisition ZEISS Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (Germany); scanned at 3 kV accelerating voltage under different magnifications
EDX 0.45 keV (Ti); 0.52 keV (O); elemental mapping of C, O, and Ti strongly confirmed successful incorporation and dispersion of TiO₂ within the biofilm
EDX acquisition An integrated EDX detector measured elemental composition to confirm TiO₂ presence on the biofilm surface
Mechanical properties Tensile strength = 4.6 MPa; elongation at break = 232.73 %; the source omits the space before the percent sign
Mechanical-test acquisition Universal Testing Machine, Model 1410 Titans, with a 5000 N load cell (U.K.); ASTM D882 with slight modification; sample dimensions 100 × 78 mm; grip separation 50 mm; test speed 50 mm/min; maximum stress at break was recorded
DSC Glass transition appeared broadened and slightly shifted toward higher temperatures over 100–140 °C, suggesting restricted polymer chain mobility; T(g) = 122 °C; the later computational overview instead describes the elevated T(g) as 105–122 °C; broadening of the T(g) peak indicated possible ester crosslinking between PVA and citric acid, further influenced by TiO₂ nanoparticles; the composite had an endothermic peak at T(m) = 221.79 °C; T(d) = 318–321 °C
DSC acquisition PerkinElmer DSC 4000 single-furnace instrument, periodically calibrated with indium and zinc using a two-point calibration at 10 °C/min; approximately 9 mg of sample heated from 20 to 350 °C at 19.8 °C/min under N₂ atmosphere against an empty reference pan; glass-transition, melting, and decomposition temperatures were recorded
Swelling The WCF biofilm reached 83 % water uptake; uptake rose steadily over the first 5 and 15 min, then slowed unevenly as the film approached equilibrium; the source omits the space before the percent sign
Swelling acquisition Biofilm samples were dipped in 50 mL distilled water and weighed at 10 min intervals starting from 5 min until constant weight was obtained; excess water was blotted before each weighing; swelling percentage = (W − W₀)/W₀ × 100 %, where W and W₀ represent the swelled and dry weights of the biofilm, respectively

FTIR 3291 cm⁻¹ (–OH stretching); 2925 cm⁻¹ (C–H stretching); 1430 cm⁻¹ (CH₂ symmetric bending, commonly associated with the crystalline structure of cellulose); 1330 cm⁻¹ (C–C and C–O skeletal vibrations of the polysaccharide backbone); two distinct bands at 1176 and 1111 cm⁻¹ (C–O antisymmetric bridge stretching and anhydroglucose ring asymmetric stretching, respectively); 1050 cm⁻¹ (strong absorption peak, C–O–C skeletal vibration of the pyranose ring); no notable peak at 1512, 1240, or 1735 cm⁻¹, which would be attributed to residual lignin and hemicellulose, confirming successful cellulose extraction
