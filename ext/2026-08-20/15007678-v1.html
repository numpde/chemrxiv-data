Platinum-coated polystyrene particles
UV–vis (1–5 h) Continuous absorbance at 215 nm for 1 h, a shift to 216 nm for 2 h, then a return to 215 nm over 3–5 h
Zeta potential (0.7 mL sample in 1 mM NaCl) Shift from +46 ± 3 mV to −54 ± 1.5 to −59 ± 2.6 mV
XPS survey (after 3 h) Pt 4f was detected; definite Pt 4f peaks at approximately 71–75 eV and distinct C 1s and O 1s peaks from the polystyrene matrix
FESEM (×70,000–×80,000, SEI, 10 kV), 1 h The PS particle surfaces remain mostly smooth and exhibit no discernible Pt nanoparticle coverage
FESEM (×70,000–×80,000, SEI, 10 kV), 2 h Small, distinct Pt nanoparticles sparsely dispersed over the PS surface begin to appear; the coating is highly uneven, with rough textures and aggregated clusters
FESEM (×70,000–×80,000, SEI, 10 kV), 3 h The Pt/PS particles showed a smoother surface than the particles obtained after 2 h
FESEM (×70,000–×80,000, SEI, 10 kV), 4 and 5 h As the reaction continued to 4 and 5 hours, coating homogeneity decreased, with irregular Pt patches and localized agglomerates reappearing on the PS surface
EDX, Pt content over time 1 h: 0 %; 2 h: approximately 0.2 wt%; 3 h: 0.1 wt%; 4 and 5 h: approximately 0.1 wt%
AFM (3 h) Uniform distribution of platinum nanoparticles
HR-TEM (300 kV, 3 h) Decoration of platinum nanoparticles on the surface of the polymer particles was confirmed
UV–vis/Tauc plot, apparent band gap over 1–5 h A slight decrease was generally observed as the coating duration increased from 1 to 5 h
UV–vis/Tauc plot reporting qualifier The apparent band gap obtained from the optical measurements does not, by itself, establish the positions of the conduction and valence bands or confirm photo-induced electron–hole generation
