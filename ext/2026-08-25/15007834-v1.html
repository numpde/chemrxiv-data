Peroxide-containing ethanol
Ti(IV) sulfate colorimetry, evaporation Concentrating 2 mL of ethanol to 200 μL resulted in a peroxide concentration as high as 3.26 mM
Ti(IV) sulfate colorimetry, heating At 65 °C in the presence of oxygen, the peroxide concentration increased monotonically over time, reaching an increment of 111 μM within one week
EPR, S-H ethanol evaporation residue After dropping S-H ethanol onto a glass substrate and allowing it to stand for 15 min, a robust characteristic four-line (1:2:2:1) DMPO-OH signal emerged
EPR, ethanol–water solution A freshly prepared 25 % (v/v) ethanol–water solution exhibited co-existing DMPO-OH and DMPO-R signals; standing for 3 days or heating at 45 °C significantly increased signal intensity
EPR, FeSO₄ addition Introducing 200 μM FeSO₄ solution into peroxide-containing ethanol in a 1:1 ratio caused immediate intense ·OH and ·R signals; HO₂· signals were detected within 5 min, and all three radical-adduct intensities increased over 15 min
EPR, K-E ethanol At a peroxide concentration of 62.22 μM, heating and Fe²⁺ addition yielded analogous signals with lower intensities; no EPR signals were detected under these conditions with peroxide-free ethanol
EPR, 5 % (v/v) ethanol–water mixture DMPO-OH and DMPO-R signals were observed after Fe²⁺ addition
