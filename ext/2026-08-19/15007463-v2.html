Identifier 3a
Cyclic voltammetry, oxidative scan of 10 mM 3a in 100 mM TBAPF₆ in DMF at 20 mV s⁻¹ Single, irreversible oxidation feature with an anodic peak current of +0.30 V vs Fc/Fc⁺; the feature was attributed to the single-electron oxidation of 3a to the corresponding (Niᴵᴵᴵ)⁺ complex 4a
Cyclic voltammetry, oxidative scans at 20–2000 mV s⁻¹ No reversibility was observed at any of the scan rates tested; no reoxidation feature was observed
Cyclic voltammetry with 0–0.75 equivalents TBACl in 100 mM TBAPF₆ in DMF at 20 mV s⁻¹ Completely irreversible trace with no observable Niᴵᴵᴵ return reduction feature, together with an increasingly larger cathodic shift of the peak as more TBACl was added
Cyclic voltammetry with more than 0.75 equivalents TBACl in 100 mM TBAPF₆ in DMF at 20 mV s⁻¹ Direct oxidation of the chloride anion began to dominate the CV
Cyclic voltammetry with and without added chloride in 100 mM TBAPF₆ in DMF, current normalized with respect to the square root of the scan rate A catalytic current appeared; higher normalized currents were observed at slower scan rates; a cathodic shift of the peak current was observed as the scan rate decreased
Cyclic voltammetry, oxidative scans at different nickel concentrations A linear relationship between the peak current and the nickel concentration was observed
Cyclic voltammetry, oxidative scan with excess aryl chloride 10a in 100 mM TBAPF₆ in DMF at 20 and 150 mV s⁻¹, current normalized with respect to scan rate A decrease in peak current was observed; the same behavior was observed when the study was repeated with 1 equivalent TBACl
Cyclic voltammetry, reductive scan of 5 mM 3a in 100 mM NBu₄PF₆ in DMF at 150 mV s⁻¹ Re₂, the initial reduction of 3a, was assigned to neutral Niᴵ(Ar) complex 12; the only reversible feature, Re₃/Ox₃, was assigned to Niᴵᴵ(Ar)₂ complex 13; the irreversible Re₁ feature was characteristic of NiᴵᴵCl₂ complex 18; Ox₂ and Ox₁ captured the reoxidation of Niᴵ complexes 12 and 14, respectively
Cyclic voltammetry, reductive scan with 20 equivalents aryl chloride 10a or aryl bromide 10b in 100 mM TBAPF₆ in DMF at 150 mV s⁻¹ All previously observed redox features collapsed into a single new irreversible catalytic wave at more reducing potentials; Re₁, Re₂, and Re₃/Ox₃ disappeared completely; the new feature was tentatively assigned to a neutral Niᴵ species
Square-wave voltammetry with 20 equivalents aryl bromide 10b in 100 mM NBu₄PF₆ in DMF The redox features observed without aryl halide were almost completely suppressed; small residual signals attributable to Niᴵᴵ(Ar)Cl and Niᴵᴵ(Ar)₂ remained detectable at lower nickel concentrations; the dominant feature was a new irreversible reduction wave that increased in intensity and shifted to more cathodic potentials as the nickel concentration increased; the wave corresponded to the peak previously assigned to a neutral Niᴵ species; the signal broadened at higher nickel concentrations
