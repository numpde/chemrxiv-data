PGF and conventional nanopipettes
SEM, PGF nanopipette Cone angle: 2.15°; taper length: approximately 4 mm
Confocal fluorescence microscopy, rhodamine 6G-doped pSPA A fluorescent signal extended continuously to the tip of the nanopipette, confirming that the pSPA had fully filled the lumen and formed a gel matrix free of voids
Normalized ion-current approach response, 150 mM KCl The PGF nanopipette showed a smooth and gradual decrease in ionic current beginning at approximately 2 μm from the surface; the conventional nanopipette maintained a stable current until approximately 100 nm from the surface, where the current dropped sharply; at the same distance where the conventional nanopipette current dropped by 1 %, the PGF nanopipette current dropped by 24 %
