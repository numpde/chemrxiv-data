Room-temperature phosphorescence Emission maximum: 493 nm; triplet energy: 2.76 eV, determined from the high-energy edge where the emission intensity was 10 % of the emission maximum
Emission lifetime in Ar-saturated water 2.1 µs at room temperature; independent of pH; 1.7 µs at 60 °C
Emission lifetime in air-saturated water 547 ns; addition of 0.1 M Na₂SO₃ drastically elongated the lifetime, almost restoring the value obtained under Ar saturation
Emission lifetime under hydrogen-generation conditions 2.1 µs before irradiation; 330 ns after 3 h of irradiation
Transient absorption of the ³MLCT state Broad absorption band between 400 and 550 nm
Two-pump transient absorption (405 and 515 nm, 10 ns inter-pulse delay) A new signal was assigned to hydrated electrons and oxidized IrFsppy; it remained largely constant up to 4 ns, with an early decay of ~210 ps to about 80 % of the initial value
Two-pump transient absorption, second-pump wavelength dependence (343 nm first pump, 450–1000 nm second pump, 10 ns separation) Wavelengths from 600 to 1000 nm did not trigger hydrated-electron formation; below ~600 nm the hydrated-electron signal increased rapidly and peaked at ~485 nm before decreasing
Photoionization quantum yield initiated by the green laser pulse 4.5 %
Hydrated-electron lifetime (35 µM IrFsppy, 0.15 M NaH₂PO₄, strongly alkaline solution, pH ~12) 2.3 µs in the absence of protons
Hydrated-electron quenching by protons, Stern–Volmer rate constant kq = 1.8 × 10¹⁰ M⁻¹ s⁻¹
Transient absorption of oxidized IrFsppy Pronounced ground-state bleach in the UVA region and a broad absorption band centered at 600 nm; the spectrum agreed with the spectroelectrochemical spectrum of one-electron-oxidized IrFsppy
